# Strap Shinrai's lens to the chest

Shinrai's bodycam mode should come from moving the lens off the eyes, not from stacking filters. Real police cameras are worn on the sternum about 25–35 cm below the eyes (estimate). They see through a roughly 125–140° horizontal fisheye with no stabilisation, record 30 fps H.264 at roughly 4–10 Mbps, and adjust exposure over about a second. A chest lens sits below the gun, swings with the torso instead of the head, and missed a third of the critical events that officers' eyes saw in a 2024 eye-tracking study. Bodycam (Reissad) and Unrecord made their names on exactly this behaviour: a gun that drifts off frame centre, blown-out skies, exposure that hunts. The post-process kit itself is already sold as a commodity on Fab. Reviewers rarely objected to the filters. They objected to sway, recoil readability and motion sickness, so every motion layer needs its own 0–100% slider from day one. In Godot 4.7 Forward+ the whole look fits in a small pipeline. A rectilinear source camera with overscan is held at 30 Hz in a SubViewport. One lens pass fuses the equidistant remap, rolling shutter, chromatic aberration and vignette, and one display-space pass handles sharpening, compression, banding and grain. The plan below adds this as a switchable mode beside the current eye-height ADS, which stays the default. It splits Shinrai's camera into an "eye" that aims and a "lens" that records.

## Real bodycams are 130° fisheye chests recording 30 fps at 4–10 Mbps

Current duty cameras are very similar to each other. The Motorola V700 publishes **130° H / 73° V / 149° diagonal at 16:9, 1080p/720p/480p, 5–30 fps, H.264 High Profile, under 0.1 lux, with an HDR mode and optional "vertical dewarping (removes fisheye effect)"** ([V700 datasheet](https://www.motorolasolutions.com/content/dam/msi/docs/products/body-worn-cameras/v700_datasheet.pdf)). The Axon Body 4 runs **~140° / 76° / ~160° in 16:9 or 127° / 93° in 4:3 on a 5 MP sensor, 30 fps** ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/resources/how-axon-body-cameras-bridge-the-gap); [AB4 card](https://cdn.mediavalet.com/usva/axon/lRL_NrK760OIbgl2z9W-bA/ixJhWEJZgU2E2WMqwDxsOw/Original/Axon%20Body%204%20Product%20Card%20Enterprise%20EN_GB.pdf)). Agencies can set it to 120° or 160° diagonal ([Axon AB4 settings](https://www.axon.com/help/axon-body-4/cameras-and-sensors/body/settings.htm)). The Axon Body 3 and Body Mini sit at **125.2° / 68.6° / 146.4°**, with 720H as the Mini's default resolution ([Axon Body Mini specs](https://cdn.mediavalet.com/usva/axon/ZcSXZCI2DUiwgseIubG-5w/Ea0M4hsKZUeH3BIw-3PhjA/Original/Axon%20Body%20Mini%20Specs%20-%2018%20May%202026%2C%20EN_US.pdf)). The DHS market survey confirms the pattern. Every surveyed camera is progressive scan, 30 fps is the common rate, and the Axon units use a **fixed-focus, all-glass lens**. Stabilisation is the exception: only the i-PRO BWC4000 (6-axis gyro) and Pro-Vision (EIS) advertise it ([DHS SAVER](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2024-02/24_02_29_st_bodyworncamerasmsr_1.pdf)).

The published angles show the lens type. A rectilinear lens at 130° H in 16:9 would imply about 101° V and 132° diagonal. Motorola's 130/73/149 instead matches an **equidistant (f-theta) fisheye** almost exactly: 130/73 equals the 16:9 aspect, and √(130² + 73²) = 149.1°. The Body 3 and VB400 figures fit an equisolid mapping within half a degree. This is a derivation from vendor numbers, not a published lens model, but it settles the game recipe: render rectilinear, then remap radially. Engine FOV alone cannot produce this look. Axon's own **~14 pixels per degree at 1080p** (against ≥60 for the fovea) works out to 1920 px spread evenly over ~140°, which is again fisheye behaviour ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/resources/how-axon-body-cameras-bridge-the-gap)). On screen, straight lines bow outward, distortion grows toward the edges, and subjects near the edge look thinner ([Amped](https://blog.ampedsoftware.com/2021/02/02/lens-distortion-cameras-may-change-the-shape-of-things)). A Police1 demo found an Axon Body 4 "significantly misrepresented" distance against a 50 mm reference ([Police1](https://www.police1.com/officer-safety/uncovering-the-hidden-distortions-in-body-worn-camera-footage)). One caution comes from a single secondary citation: manufacturers claim 120–170° but one test measured 72–114° ([Murray et al. 2024 record](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/15614263.2024.2328664)). Treat the vendor figures as optimistic.

No vendor publishes bitrates, but storage tables imply them. Axon Mini 1080H uses 4.5 GB/h (≈10 Mbps) and 720H uses 2.0 GB/h (≈4.4 Mbps) ([Axon Body Mini specs](https://cdn.mediavalet.com/usva/axon/ZcSXZCI2DUiwgseIubG-5w/Ea0M4hsKZUeH3BIw-3PhjA/Original/Axon%20Body%20Mini%20Specs%20-%2018%20May%202026%2C%20EN_US.pdf)). The V700 stores 28 h of 1080p on 128 GB, also ≈10 Mbps ([V700 datasheet](https://www.motorolasolutions.com/content/dam/msi/docs/products/body-worn-cameras/v700_datasheet.pdf)). The sensor itself is undocumented: nobody names the part or confirms rolling shutter. A forensic consultant does name rolling shutter as the cause of the "jello", skew and light-banding artefacts in law-enforcement video ([Forensic VR](https://medium.com/forensic-vr/rolling-shutter-problems-and-solutions-for-law-enforcement-cameras-part-1-9d8bddb35d4b)), and Amped calls shake in body-worn footage a common occurrence ([Amped 2015](https://blog.ampedsoftware.com/2015/06/24/body-worn-cameras-bwcs-as-a-silver-bullet-vol-2-quality-2)). Low light stays colour on Axon, Motorola and Getac. IR monochrome is a budget-brand feature ([DHS SAVER](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2024-02/24_02_29_st_bodyworncamerasmsr_1.pdf)). Axon markets "motion blur correction", which concedes that blur is a known problem ([Axon comparison](https://a.storyblok.com/f/133181/x/5d21d52e94/ab2-ab3-ab4-comparison.PDF)). Exposure is the other signature. Axon says the camera re-adapts "typically in a second or so" ([Axon](https://www.axon.com/resources/how-axon-body-cameras-bridge-the-gap)), and Force Science warns that bright-to-dark transitions can briefly blank the image entirely ([Police1 / Force Science](https://www.police1.com/police-products/body-cameras/articles/10-limitations-of-body-cams-you-need-to-know-for-your-protection-Y0Lhpm3vlPTsJ9OZ/)).

Mounting defaults to the centre chest: shirt clip, magnet or MOLLE on the vest, with shoulder, epaulette, helmet and glasses mounts as alternatives ([DHS SAVER](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2024-02/24_02_29_st_bodyworncamerasmsr_1.pdf); [VB400 datasheet](https://www.motorolasolutions.com/content/dam/msi/docs/products/body-worn-cameras/vb400-datasheet-na.pdf)). Axon sells a tethered POV module for head, helmet or Oakley-glasses mounting "for eye-level recording" ([Axon Flex POV Oakley guide](https://cdn.mediavalet.com/usva/axon/eyX2ZM3ESEqqIahc5UXMDg/MXHbVfFoK0q1AQgzp57Ctw/Original/Axon%20Flex%20POV%20Oakley%20Flak%202%20Mount%20Installation%20Guide%20EN_US.pdf)). No source measures lens height. A sternum lens on an average adult sits roughly 1.30–1.45 m up, which is an estimate. The burned-in overlay is generic across brands: **date, time and serial number in the upper-right corner, updating every frame, in agency local time or UTC, with "MUTED" shown when audio is muted** ([Axon AB4 settings](https://www.axon.com/help/axon-body-4/cameras-and-sensors/body/settings.htm); [Axon Recorded On overlay](https://www.axon.com/help/axon-evidence/software/axon-evidence/evidence/review-evidence/recorded-on-overlay.htm)). Getac adds an officer ID ([Getac BC-04 sheet](https://www.getac.com/content/dam/getac/all-sites-img/Getac_BC-04_Product_US_250116.pdf)). Font, colour and the exact string format are undocumented, so Shinrai's invented overlay is free to differ.

Audio has two well-documented signatures. The camera face carries two to four MEMS mics, for example one forward and one side-facing on the V700 to fight wind ([V700 datasheet](https://www.motorolasolutions.com/content/dam/msi/docs/products/body-worn-cameras/v700_datasheet.pdf)), so the wearer's voice and clothing dominate. The cameras also loop-record continuously, and Axon's pre-event buffer **defaults to 30 s, is configurable 0–120 s, and is video-only by default** ([Axon AB4 operation](https://www.axon.com/help/axon-body-4/cameras-and-sensors/body/operation.htm); [AB4 card](https://cdn.mediavalet.com/usva/axon/lRL_NrK760OIbgl2z9W-bA/ixJhWEJZgU2E2WMqwDxsOw/Original/Axon%20Body%204%20Product%20Card%20Enterprise%20EN_GB.pdf)). NYPD runs 60 s video-only ([NYPD BWC policy](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nypd/downloads/pdf/public_information/post-final/body-worn-cameras-nypd-Impact-and-use-policy_2.4.26_final.pdf)). An August 2026 Anchorage shooting clip had no audio for exactly this reason ([Alaska's News Source](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2026/08/15/police-chief-no-audio-recorded-body-cam-footage-officer-involved-shooting/)). Released footage therefore often opens mid-action in silence, then cuts to sound at the activation frame.

## A chest lens sits below the gun and misses a third of what the eyes see

The camera's position, not its image quality, decides what footage of an armed person looks like. Force Science warns that when a chest-camera wearer fires, the camera "may not record much more than your extended arms and hands." Blading the stance turns the lens off the threat, and everything "from your nose to your hands" can block it ([Police1 / Force Science](https://www.police1.com/police-products/body-cameras/articles/10-limitations-of-body-cams-you-need-to-know-for-your-protection-Y0Lhpm3vlPTsJ9OZ/)). Murray, Lewinski et al. put 44 officers in eye-tracking glasses with a chest GoPro standing in for a bodycam. The officers looked at about 80% of critical events, but **the chest camera captured 66.2%, under 48% of key events, and the suspect's shooting only about 46% of the time** ([Tactical Science summary](https://tacticalscience.substack.com/p/the-eyes-have-it); [T&F DOI](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/15614263.2024.2328664)). Suss and Petushek's live-fire drills with pistol, rifle and shotgun found full target visibility in about 80% of frames for both head and chest mounts. Gun-mounted cameras managed 68% and shoulder cameras about 12%. Before firing, head mounts saw the target ~70% of the time against 14–41% for every other mount ([Michigan Tech abstract](https://digitalcommons.mtu.edu/michigantech-p/14889)). The head moves first in turns, so head cameras catch the target sooner, and raising the arms can shove a torso or shoulder camera aside ([AIP Inside Science](https://www.aip.org/inside-science/the-science-behind-where-police-should-place-their-body-cameras)).

None of these studies measured how much of the frame the gun fills, so the following framing is geometric inference. With the lens ~25–35 cm below the eye line, an isosceles pistol presentation puts the hands and slide in the **upper-middle of the frame**, with the forearms rising from the lower corners in a V. The sights never line up, because the lens is not on the sight line. A shouldered rifle runs diagonally from the strong-side lower corner toward centre, with the optic seen from below. Low ready puts the muzzle at the bottom edge. Compressed ready puts the hands just above or beside the lens, often out of frame. A Weaver or bladed torso rotates 30–45° toward the strong side, pointing the whole image off-axis. A glasses or helmet lens 3–10 cm beside the eye restores a near-classic FPS view with a slightly parallaxed sight picture. During a draw the gun is mostly invisible to a chest lens and appears only at press-out. The timing of that is well measured. In a 148-officer AZPOST study, **cold draw-to-first-shot averaged 1.777 ± 0.329 s** ([VirTra](https://www.virtra.com/time-analysis-of-a-certified-peace-officers-blog/)). From a ready position, Force Science measured **0.64 s for low ready (unaimed) and 0.83 s for high ready (aimed)** ([Police1](https://www.police1.com/officer-safety/articles/how-much-do-finger-placement-and-ready-position-matter-aVMZMe7Xh9wqJB2d)). Under recoil the chest image barely moves, because the camera rides the torso and not the gun. What moves is the gun in frame: a quick muzzle flick and a few small bounces as the slide closes ([Lucky Gunner](https://www.luckygunner.com/lounge/recoil-lets-talk-about-feelings/amp/)). A muzzle flash can fall between 30 fps frames entirely ([Police1 / Force Science](https://www.police1.com/police-products/body-cameras/articles/10-limitations-of-body-cams-you-need-to-know-for-your-protection-Y0Lhpm3vlPTsJ9OZ/)).

Gait biomechanics supply real numbers for camera motion, with one important twist. The head is actively stabilised in pitch against vertical bob by vestibulocollic reflexes ([Hirasaki 1999](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s002210050781)). A chest camera gets none of that correction, so its footage looks shakier than what the wearer sees. Two other points shape the turning behaviour. Transverse (yaw) rotation of the thorax in walking spans only about 3–5° ([BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders](https://bmcmusculoskeletdisord.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2474-14-345)). During 180° turns the head leads the thorax by a peak of 13.2 ± 11.0° at about 194 °/s ([PMC7249140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7249140)). So the chest lens should trail the player's look by roughly that much.

Two inconsistencies matter for tuning. Running vertical oscillation *falls* as speed rises while lateral sway grows, so a sprint should look wider, not just bigger. The Frontiers paper's own discussion quotes slightly different figures from its results table, and reported head-pitch amplitudes range from 1.5° to 9° depending on whether a study counts peak or peak-to-peak. No source measured sternum motion directly, the "tactical" bent-knee walk with a gun at aim, or the effect of a 9–14 kg duty load. Shinrai's values for those are estimates.

## Genre hits sell behaviour, and players punish sway and sickness

Bodycam (Reissad, UE5) defines the commercial genre. It has no crosshair, ammo counter or HUD, an in-world tablet in place of menus, a "True Body" rig that physically links arms, torso, legs and weapon, ragdoll stumbling, and separate calm and stressed reload states. The studio says it studied real footage for optics, camera movement, compression, exposure and sound ([Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2406770/Bodycam/)). Reviewers observed that **weapons move slightly independently of the view and keep swaying in ADS**, and that ADS holds the sights far from the camera rather than snapping them to the line of sight. That keeps the gun from covering half the screen ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/bodycam-early-access-review-through-the-looking-glass); [SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/bodycams-unique-perspective-demands-your-attention/)). Death is diegetic: the body slumps and the feed cuts ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/bodycam-early-access-review-through-the-looking-glass)). The September 2026 v0.8 #2 patch was still adjusting weapon kick to reduce camera clipping and smoothing camera transitions two years into Early Access ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/25126576/)). The camera is never finished. Bodycam sold about 800k units by July 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodycam_(video_game))) and in November 2026 stood at 79% positive across 38,226 English reviews and 86% across recent ones ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2406770/Bodycam/)). Specific Bodycam settings (FOV lock, fisheye or chromatic-aberration sliders, deadzone size) could not be verified. Only a Film Grain option surfaced in patch summaries ([Patchbot](https://patchbot.io/games/bodycam)).

Unrecord went viral in 2023 because viewers believed it was real footage. The developer posted a noclip video to prove it was real-time UE5 ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/unrecord-dev-posts-noclip-video-to-prove-the-realistic-bodycam-fps-isnt-fake)). Commentators credited camera *failures*: a sky washed out to white by limited dynamic range, and exposure that visibly shifts ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/unrecord-took-internet-by-storm/)). The noclip proof worked because free flight broke the camera's physicality. The realism lived in the camera model, not the asset fidelity. Players also flagged the cost: iron sights that looked far away and shaky ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2381520/discussions/0/3827539207862294509)). Ground Branch is the clearest documented reference for body-linked first person. The view is tied to the body, free-look is separate from aim, the free-aim deadzone is optional and customisable, sensitivity is set separately for point, unmagnified and magnified aim, and bullets leave the muzzle rather than the screen centre ([Ground Branch](https://www.groundbranch.com/about/)). On deadzones, the recurring complaints concern the edges, not the concept. Arma 3 players report turn speed "greatly reduced" at the border ([BI Feedback Tracker](https://feedback.bistudio.com/T59128)), and Sandstorm players found its deadzone more pronounced than the original's ([NWI forum](https://forums.focus-entmt.com/post/98360)). No source publishes deadzone sizes in degrees.

The filter stack is now a commodity. Matify's Fab kit ships a body-attached camera with only the head hidden, fisheye applied to rendered overscan so the corners hold real pixels, chromatic fringe, sensor noise, grain, vignette, a REC/timestamp overlay, and switchable Bodycam, Helmet Cam and Clean True First Person profiles ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/matify-games-bodycam-camera-system-true-first-person-multiplayer-v1-0-0/2848471)). Another kit adds lens dirt, digital sharpening and stencil-masked pixelation ([Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/841b4d3b-37d1-42f7-bd03-236efecdae62?lang=tr)). An original game therefore stands apart through how body, weapon and lens move against each other, not through the filter set.

Motion sickness is the genre's most consistent criticism. Reviewers warned that the shake and gun sway could overwhelm some players ([SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/bodycams-unique-perspective-demands-your-attention/); [GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/bodycam-early-access-review-through-the-looking-glass)). One player quit after about two minutes because of the tilt when looking or strafing, and the developer promised motion-sickness controls like the camera-shake option already added ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/9582658)). The genre's core cue conflicts directly with accessibility guidance. Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117 asks games to avoid camera shake, bob and motion blur or make them disable-able, and to provide adjustable FOV ([XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)). The Game Accessibility Guidelines treat any difference between controller movement and camera movement, weapon bob and smoothing included, as something to avoid or make optional ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/avoid-or-provide-option-to-disable-any-difference-between-controller-movement-and-camera-movement/)). Body lag and free aim are exactly such differences, so each needs its own slider down to zero. Readability is the other cost: reviewers found friend and foe hard to tell apart through the bodycam image ([SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/bodycams-unique-perspective-demands-your-attention/)).

## Godot can fuse lens, rolling shutter and frame hold into two passes

**Lens.** Render a normal rectilinear Camera3D at a wider FOV than the output (overscan). Then, for every output pixel, invert the fisheye mapping to a view direction and project that direction back into the source. For a radial lens the azimuth is preserved, θ = g⁻¹(r·g(θ_h)), and for equidistant g(θ) = θ ([Wikipedia: Fisheye lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheye_lens)). The corner of a 16:9 frame sits at normalised radius √(1 + 1/a²) = 1.147. Avoiding black corners requires **tan θ_src_h ≥ tan θ_c · a/√(a² + 1)**, where θ_c is the corner angle. Sizing for the side edges alone (tan θ_src_h ≥ tan θ_h) instead lets the corners fall to a dark "lens housing", which also suits the look. The rule has a steep cost: the centre is magnified by tan θ_src_h / θ_h, so the image softens there as the target FOV grows. The table applies the derived rule to Shinrai's 16:9 frame. Camera3D's `fov` is vertical under the default KEEP_HEIGHT; Shinrai's own scope script sets KEEP_HEIGHT explicitly in `C:\Users\hp\OneDrive\Documenti\GitHub\shinrai\scripts\extraction_prism_scope_view.gd`.

Interestingly, a 130° × 73° equidistant lens keeps exactly the 73° vertical coverage Shinrai already uses. In the camera setup in `C:\Users\hp\OneDrive\Documenti\GitHub\shinrai\scripts\player.gd` (line 329), `camera.fov = 73.0` gives about 105.5° horizontally at 16:9. Switching modes therefore keeps the vertical extent and adds about 25° of horizontal periphery, with barrel curvature. The softened centre can be offset with `Viewport.scaling_3d_scale` around 1.3–1.5, at roughly scale² cost. In Godot the lens pass has three possible homes. A CanvasLayer ColorRect reading `hint_screen_texture` (or a SubViewport texture) is the simplest. A `CompositorEffect` compute shader is the most correct: Forward+ only, using `texture_copy` to a temp texture before gathering, since a warp cannot read and write the same image ([Godot docs: The Compositor](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/compositor.html)). A 3D spatial screen-read quad is wrong, because it misses transparent objects ([Godot docs: Screen-reading shaders](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/screen-reading_shaders.html)). The notes assume, without verifying, that the compositor's POST_TRANSPARENT stage runs before tonemap. Panini projection keeps verticals straight and reads as "wide but undistorted" up to about 130° ([Hugin manual](https://hugin.sourceforge.io/docs/manual/The_General_Panini_Projection.html)). That makes it a good comfort option and the wrong choice for authenticity.

**The lens pass also handles chromatic aberration, vignette and rolling shutter.** Lateral chromatic aberration is a per-channel warp ([Wikipedia: Distortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distortion_(optics))). Scale θ by (1 ± ca) for R and B and weight it by r². Vignette uses cos⁴θ on the θ the pass already computes. Rolling shutter fits in the same pass. Each sensor row exposes at t₀ + t_readout·y/h ([USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10771699)), so rotate each output row's direction by −ω·(row − 0.5)·t_readout before projecting into the source. ω is the camera's angular velocity, measured on the CPU between held frames. The output row is the right input because the sensor sits behind the lens. Fast yaw then leans verticals and roll curves the horizon at almost no cost. Allow about 5° of extra source margin for the skew. Readout time is unsourced. 15–30 ms is an estimate.

**Display-space pass.** Order these to match a camera's DSP and then its encoder: luma-only unsharp mask, then black crush and lift with a hard highlight clip, then compression, then grain. The full 8×8 DCT is cheap as a compute shader with one workgroup per block, and is the most expensive effect in a typical stack ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/compression-artifacts-explained-dct-compute-shader)). It has to run after tonemap, though, so phase one should use a fragment approximation in the final canvas pass. That approximation combines detail-adaptive 8×8 block averaging, 4:2:0 chroma taken per 2×2 block, and luma quantised to 48–96 levels. Quality is time-modulated by a GOP phase (crisp just after each "keyframe", then degrading) and by angular speed, so pans starve the bitrate. The Godot Retro pack's JPEG shader is a ready starting point ([godotretro](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fahopness%2Fgodotretro)). Grain should come from an integer hash seeded by the held-frame index and generated at half resolution, so that compression smears it.

**Exposure and frame rate.** `CameraAttributesPractical` provides Forward+ auto exposure with `auto_exposure_speed` (default 0.5), `auto_exposure_scale` (default 0.4) and min/max sensitivity clamps (max default 800 ISO). Its adaptation is exponential and never overshoots ([CameraAttributes](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_cameraattributes.html); [CameraAttributesPractical](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_cameraattributespractical.html)). Highlights only clip if the tonemapper lets them. Shinrai's newer scenes use AGX (`C:\Users\hp\OneDrive\Documenti\GitHub\shinrai\scripts\midori_park_size_blockout.gd`, line 483), which rolls highlights off gracefully, so the bodycam camera needs its own Environment with Linear or low-white Filmic tonemapping. For a 30 fps look, render the world into a SubViewport kept on `UPDATE_DISABLED` and set it to `UPDATE_ONCE` on an exact 1/30 s accumulator ([Godot docs: SubViewport](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_subviewport.html)). Input, physics and audio keep running at full rate. `Engine.max_fps` is the wrong tool because it also throttles input. The viewmodel must render inside the same SubViewport so it holds too. Grain seed, rolling-shutter ω and GOP phase should advance only on held frames.

**Procedural motion.** Treat the bodycam transform as a stack: body yaw lagging the aim, then the chest mount offset, then footstep bob phase-locked to distance, then impact springs, then trauma shake. Use exponential decay (`1 − exp(−λΔt)`) or critically damped springs throughout, so behaviour is frame-rate independent. Shake follows Eiserloh's trauma model: magnitude = trauma², linear decay, a separate noise offset per axis ([StraySpark camera docs](https://www.strayspark.studio/docs/godot-camera-system/noise-and-shake); [Bevy example](https://bevy.org/examples/camera/2d-screen-shake/)). The existing bob cannot be reused. As written, `bob_phase += horizontal_speed * delta * 1.8` (`player.gd` line 1452) feeds `sin(bob_phase * 5.5)` and `abs(cos(bob_phase * 5.5))` (line 2031). At the 3.85 m/s walk speed (line 113) that gives about 6 Hz lateral and 12 Hz vertical, three to six times human step frequency. It reads as small viewmodel jitter, not gait. The walk speed itself is a real-world jog of 13.9 km/h. Bodycam gait should therefore drive cadence from movement *state* through a speed-to-cadence curve (walk ≈ 1.9 Hz, sprint ≈ 2.8 Hz), not from a physical stride length.

**Free aim, sway and recoil.** Keep the aim as state separate from the lens. Mouse input moves the aim inside an elliptical deadzone, and the excess beyond its edge pushes the body. A soft, eased border avoids the sticky edge Arma players complain about. Weapon sway is a spring driven by mouse velocity and body acceleration. Recoil goes into underdamped weapon springs plus a little camera trauma, and the chest image should not follow the muzzle climb. The hit ray must come from the muzzle along the weapon's forward vector, as Ground Branch does, since the screen centre no longer means anything.

**True body and the viewmodel.** Godot 4.6 brought IK back: `TwoBoneIK3D` is deterministic and needs a pole target, `LookAtModifier3D` (4.4) handles spine aim, and `LimitAngularVelocityModifier3D` smooths snaps ([Godot blog](https://godotengine.org/article/inverse-kinematics-returns-to-godot-4-6/); [TwoBoneIK3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_twoboneik3d.html)). A `BoneAttachment3D` on the chest bone, followed through a short exp-decay filter rather than parented rigidly, gives authentic body-driven motion. The head mesh can be hidden from the camera while still casting shadows. Shinrai's separate viewmodel layer is workable as long as it stays inside the same 3D render that the lens pass distorts. If a separate viewmodel image is composited after the lens pass, the gun stays undistorted and the look breaks. A projection-override viewmodel shader needs its depth squash flipped for 4.3's reverse-Z ([Godot blog: Reverse Z](https://godotengine.org/article/introducing-reverse-z/)), and it misplaces weapon shadows ([Godot Forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/fixing-weapon-clipping-using-shader-causes-shadow-bug/91595)). In bodycam mode any viewmodel FOV must be tuned for its size *after* the warp. Otherwise the very wide source FOV shrinks the gun to a toy.

## A four-phase plan keeps current ADS as default and adds the lens beside it

The central refactor is to split the current head node into an **Eye**, which stays at 1.58 m, owns aim, anchors the viewmodel and receives the lean, and a **Lens**, which renders. In default mode the two coincide and nothing changes. In bodycam mode the Lens drops to a chest mount and lags the Eye. The gun then naturally appears high in the frame, seen from below, with no sight picture, as it does in real chest footage, without re-authoring per-gun hip or ADS poses. The mode adds no new FOV zoom on ADS, because real duty lenses are fixed ([DHS SAVER](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/2024-02/24_02_29_st_bodyworncamerasmsr_1.pdf)). The `camera.fov` lerp toward `ads_fov` at `player.gd` line 1423 is therefore bypassed in this mode. ADS instead raises the gun toward the Eye line at an extended distance, giving the Bodycam-style "sights held far, not snapped" picture. A **Head** mount profile, with the lens beside the eye, gives a playable sight picture and less shake, since the head is vestibular-stabilised. Suss's data supports offering it: head mounts see the target before firing far more often than chest mounts.

**Phase 0, the rig split, comes with a regression gate.** Add the Eye/Lens split, the mode toggle, the settings scaffold and a BodycamProfile resource. Acceptance is a pixel-identical capture in default mode through the existing `C:\Users\hp\OneDrive\Documenti\GitHub\shinrai\tests\weapon_ads_capture.gd` harness, plus same-camera before/after stills of the new mode. **Phase 1 adds the mount, lens and motion:** chest mount, equidistant lens in a canvas pass, body-yaw lag, distance-locked gait bob, strafe and turn roll, lean coupling, and bodycam ADS. **Phase 2 adds the sensor:** the 30 Hz SubViewport hold, rolling shutter and chromatic aberration fused into the lens pass, auto exposure with Linear tonemapping, sharpening, compression, banding, grain, and the burned-in overlay drawn *before* the compression pass so it blurs with the image. **Phase 3 covers weapon behaviour:** free-aim deadzone, sway that persists into ADS, recoil springs that leave the image alone, a muzzle-origin hit ray, weapon-obstruction pull-back, and 0–1 frame muzzle flashes. **Phase 4 is true body and extras:** a full-body mesh with `TwoBoneIK3D` arms and a `LookAtModifier3D` spine, a filtered chest `BoneAttachment3D`, a head that casts shadows while hidden from the camera, MEMS-mic audio character, a 30 s pre-event replay that records transforms rather than frames and stays silent until activation, and optionally an HDR `CompositorEffect` lens and a true DCT compute pass.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the work sits. The bodycam look is mainly a *rig* problem, separating the point that aims from the point that records, and only secondarily a shader problem. Once the Lens trails the Eye at chest height, Shinrai gets the genre's defining cues for free: the gun riding high in frame, threats slipping out of shot during a draw or bladed stance, turns arriving late. Shaders can't fake those cues convincingly, and competitors can't buy them from a kit. Real-camera numbers also argue against two common genre exaggerations. Duty cameras do not zoom on ADS, and a chest image does not climb with the muzzle. Holding both lines keeps Shinrai's mode distinct from games that treat "bodycam" as a filter over a head camera.

The biggest risk is not authenticity but comfort. Measured chest motion is larger than a stabilised head ever shows the brain, and every authentic layer violates published accessibility guidance. So the 100%-realism values should exist mainly as a ceiling, with Balanced shipping at about 60% motion. The Head mount should be treated as a first-class profile rather than an afterthought, because the evidence says it is both more comfortable and the mount that sees the target before the shot.
