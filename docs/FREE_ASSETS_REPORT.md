# Free assets for Shinrai: research report (2026-11-12)

Every licence below was read on the source's own pages that day, and each listed asset was opened and checked a second time. Licences change: re-check the item page on the day you download, and keep the link, author, licence name and date with the file.

Status key: **OK** = licence confirmed on the item page and usable in a sold game; **OK, credit** = usable if the author is credited; **CHECK** = could not be confirmed or something was off (read the note); **NO** = do not use.

## Sources

### Poly Haven (polyhaven.com) - HDRIs, PBR textures and 3D models, all CC0. Read on 2026-11-12 from polyhaven.com/license, polyhaven.com/our-api, the public JSON API at api.polyhaven.com, and the CC0 deed at creativecommons.org.

Yes: every Poly Haven asset (HDRI, texture, model) is CC0, so it may be used, modified and shipped inside a commercially sold game, in any engine including Godot, with no credit required and no fee or revenue share. Redistribution is allowed too, so committing the files to the game repository and shipping them in the game build is fine. There is no engine restriction and no per-item licence variation: one licence covers the whole library. Credit is optional (they ask commercial users to consider supporting them on Patreon, which is a request, not a condition).

Library size today (from their API): 869 textures, 521 models, plus HDRIs.

What suits this game:
- Ground / park: terrain 131, rock 125, sand 60, gravel 13, bark 24 (useful for tree trunks), snow 8, plus aerial ground sets (30).
- City: concrete 80, plaster-concrete 136, brick 108, road 36, asphalt 20 (e.g. asphalt_01/03/05/06/07, clean_asphalt, worn_asphalt, road_damaged, road_damaged_2, asphalt_pit_lane), cobblestone 38, roofing 23, tiles 14.
- Metal: only 25, and almost all are rusty sheets, corrugated iron, shutters and container sides. Good for port, containers, shutters, fences; NOT suitable as gun finishes (no parkerised/anodised/blued steel or polymer). Gun materials need another source (e.g. ambientCG) or hand-authored PBR.
- Models: props 176, industrial 97, nature 110, plants 57, rocks 37, ground cover 32, structures 26, trees 20, buildings 13, grass 4, weapons 3 (firearms 2).
- Trees: only 20 entries and they are South African or conifer: pine/fir (pine_forest collection), jacaranda, island trees, quiver trees, Karoo shrubs, stumps and dead trunks. No Japanese species (no cherry, maple, ginkgo, zelkova, bamboo, cedar). Useful picks for a Japanese park are limited to stumps, dead trunks, roots, moss_01, fern_02, shrub_01-04, nettle, dandelion, celandine, periwinkle, weed_plant_02, grass_medium_02, and possibly the pines.
- Firearms: just two, both vintage (bolt_action_rifle_7_62, about 20k triangles; service_pistol, about 27.5k triangles). They do not match the game's existing gun list; only useful as world props or as a reference for worn-metal texturing.
- HDRIs: outdoor skies including 'pure sky' versions, useful for sky lighting.

Resolutions and sizes (measured from the API file listings):
- Textures: 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k (some scans up to 16k), each map as JPG, PNG or EXR. Example asphalt_02: diffuse JPG 0.73 MB at 1k, 3.08 MB at 2k, 11.65 MB at 4k, 38.8 MB at 8k; normal JPG 1.24 / 4.94 / 17.4 / 63 MB; combined AO-rough-metal 'arm' JPG 0.30 / 1.30 / 5.2 / 19.8 MB. A full 2k JPG set is roughly 10 MB; a 1k set roughly 2.5 MB. PNG is about 3x larger. Normal maps come in OpenGL form (nor_gl), which is what Godot expects, and the 'arm' packed map plugs straight into Godot ORM materials.
- HDRIs: 1k to 16k, .hdr and .exr. Example sky: .hdr 1.4 MB at 1k, 5.5 MB at 2k, 20.7 MB at 4k, 75 MB at 8k, 262 MB at 16k. 2k-4k .hdr is the sensible range.
- Models: blend, glTF, FBX, USD, with textures at 1k-8k. Small props are light: shrub_02 glTF totals about 2 MB at 1k and 4.8 MB at 2k; service_pistol glTF about 9.4 MB at 2k.

**Risks:** Licence risk is about as low as it gets; the traps are practical, not legal.

1. Trees and big plants are far too heavy as downloaded. Triangle counts: jacaranda_tree 312k, tree_small_02 4.65 million, island_tree_01 3.7 million, fir_tree_01 7.85 million, pine_tree_01 17.4 million, grass_medium_02 1.04 million, celandine_01 1.75 million. These are film/offline-render assets (many are tagged 'geometry nodes', a Blender-only feature). They would need heavy reduction and hand-made LODs in Blender before use in an 8 GB game.
2. The repository's 100 MB single-file limit is broken by tree downloads: jacaranda_tree's glTF .bin is 208 MB and its FBX 132 MB, even at 1k textures. Do not commit these raw.
3. The API has a 'lods' flag on many models, but the file listings I opened showed no separate LOD files in the glTF/FBX downloads; LODs may exist only inside the .blend. Assume no ready-made game LODs.
4. Species mismatch: vegetation is South African (Karoo, quiver trees, jacaranda) and European-style conifers. Nothing is recognisably Japanese, so the library cannot dress a Tokyo park on its own.
5. Gun materials: the metal category will not give realistic firearm finishes. The two gun models are vintage and are not any of the game's weapons.
6. Trademarks/patents are not covered by CC0. Poly Haven models are generally unbranded, but check any prop for visible logos or real brand markings before shipping.
7. No warranty: CC0 comes with no guarantee of clearance. Poly Haven states assets are original work by its staff or contributing artists, which makes this a low risk.
8. Site vs assets: the CC0 grant covers the assets only. Preview renders, logos and site text are protected, and the site terms prohibit scraping without permission. Use the official API (free, commercial use allowed, needs a unique User-Agent header, and the API page asks that tools pulling live from it make the Poly Haven origin clear to users) or manual downloads. A one-off download of chosen assets for the game is ordinary use.
9. Memory: default download buttons often offer 4k/8k. Pick 1k-2k JPG deliberately; an 8k PNG set is 100+ MB per map and would blow both the memory budget and the 100 MB file limit.
10. Terms can change for future uploads, but CC0 is irrevocable for anything already obtained under it. Keep a note of the download date and asset page URL for each asset used.
11. AI training: the licence page sets no restriction on the assets themselves; nothing relevant to shipping a game.

**How to filter:** No licence filtering is needed: everything on the site is CC0.

To browse by category (website): https://polyhaven.com/textures/asphalt, /textures/concrete, /textures/road, /textures/terrain, /textures/bark, /textures/metal, /textures/rock; https://polyhaven.com/models/trees, /models/plants, /models/ground%20cover, /models/rocks, /models/industrial, /models/props, /models/buildings; https://polyhaven.com/hdris/outdoor (and the skies/pure-sky listings). An asset page is https://polyhaven.com/a/<asset_id>, e.g. https://polyhaven.com/a/rusty_metal_04.

On each asset page choose resolution 1K or 2K and format JPG (textures) or glTF/FBX (models) before downloading; for HDRIs choose 2K or 4K .hdr.

Machine-readable (no key, send a unique User-Agent):
- https://api.polyhaven.com/categories/textures and /categories/models for category counts
- https://api.polyhaven.com/assets?t=textures&c=asphalt (t = hdris | textures | models, c = category) returns ids, tags, polycount, max resolution
- https://api.polyhaven.com/files/<asset_id> lists every file with exact byte size and URL, so triangle count and file size can be checked BEFORE downloading. Rule of thumb for this game: reject any model whose polycount is above roughly 100k or whose .bin/.fbx is over 100 MB unless it will be reduced in Blender first.

### ambientCG (ambientcg.com) - checked live on 2026-11-12 via its own licence page, homepage, an asset page and its public API

Yes, usable. Every ambientCG asset is released under Creative Commons CC0 1.0 (public-domain dedication). That allows use in a commercially sold game, modification, and shipping the raw texture files inside the game build. There is no engine restriction of any kind, so Godot is fine. Credit is NOT required; the author says he would appreciate it and suggests the wording "Created using <asset name> from ambientCG.com, licensed under the Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal License." The licence is site-wide, not per item: I found no per-asset licence variations, and the licence page says it covers both the downloadable files and the preview renders. The CC0 waiver is written as not revocable, so files downloaded today stay CC0 even if the site later changes its terms.

**Risks:** Low-risk source; the traps are practical rather than legal.

1. No warranty and no third-party clearance. CC0 waives only the author's own copyright. It does not waive trademarks, and the author does not promise that nothing in a photo belongs to someone else. Practical rule: avoid any material, decal or atlas that shows a readable brand, logo, sign or a recognisable artwork. The atlas/decal category includes graffiti sets (e.g. GraffitiSet001); graffiti is someone else's artwork photographed, so treat those as the one category to skip or check individually.
2. Do not use the ambientCG logo or name in a way that suggests endorsement. A plain credits line is fine.
3. No AI-training clause, no engine clause, no revenue cap and no separate terms-of-use page were found; the only legal pages are the licence page and a German imprint/privacy page. Nothing to accept.
4. Change of terms: the CC0 waiver is not revocable for files already released. Keep a small record per downloaded asset (asset ID, date, licence page URL) so the provenance is provable later.
5. Size trap for this repository: 4K and above break the 100 MB single-file limit as zips (4K-JPG 80-140 MB, 8K 300-600 MB, PNG 8K about 1 GB) and are too heavy for an 8 GB card. Use 1K-JPG (about 7-10 MB per zip) or 2K-JPG (about 24-36 MB per zip); commit the unpacked maps, not the zip. Terrain OBJ files are 118 MB at the smallest setting and cannot be committed.
6. Normal map direction: packs normally carry both an OpenGL and a DirectX normal map; Godot needs the OpenGL one. Picking the wrong one makes lighting look inverted. (File naming not re-verified today, see unverified.)
7. JPG colour maps are lossy; fine for ground and walls, but roughness/normal maps show less banding from the PNG pack if a surface is seen very close (weapon materials).
8. The site shows ads and has an optional Patreon supporter login; neither changes the licence.

**How to filter:** No licence filter is needed: everything on the site is CC0. Filter only by type and keyword.

Website list pages (type values are comma-separated; sort options are Popular, Latest, Downloads, Oldest, Random, Alphabet):
- PBR surfaces: https://ambientcg.com/list?type=material,atlas,decal
- HDRI skies: https://ambientcg.com/list?type=hdri,hdri-element
- Models and terrains: https://ambientcg.com/list?type=3d-model,terrain
- Single asset page: https://ambientcg.com/view?id=Asphalt031

Public API (no key; better for the AI doing integration, because it returns exact file sizes):
- https://ambientcg.com/api/v2/full_json?type=Material&q=asphalt&limit=20&include=downloadData
- type accepts Material, HDRI, Atlas, Decal, Terrain (comma-separated); q is the keyword; numberOfResults gives the count.
- In the result pick the download attribute "1K-JPG" or "2K-JPG" and ignore 4K and above.
- The value type=3DModel did not filter (it returned the whole catalogue), so the correct API name for models is unconfirmed; use the website list page for models.

Asset ID families worth searching for this game: Asphalt, Road (with painted lines), Concrete, PavingStones, Tiles, Bricks, Metal, Planks/Wood, Bark, Ground, Fabric, LeafSet (foliage atlases), DaySkyHDRI / DayEnvironmentHDRI.

### Sketchfab (sketchfab.com) - free downloadable 3D models uploaded by users. Ownership changed: sold by Epic Games to KitBash in August 2026 (site footer now reads "KitBash SF Operations LLC"). Store is closed; free downloads still running.

There is no single Sketchfab licence: every model carries its own, chosen by the uploader. Nothing in any of them restricts the game engine, so Godot is fine.

Usable in a sold game:
- CC0 Public Domain: yes, no credit needed.
- CC Attribution (CC BY 4.0): yes, with credit. This is the bulk of the free library.
- Free Standard (Sketchfab's own licence on some free downloads): commercial use allowed, no credit needed, but the model must not be extractable as a stand-alone file. The full legal text I could open only describes a paid licence, so treat Free Standard as second choice.

Avoid:
- CC BY-SA: commercial use allowed, but modified versions must carry the same licence. How far that reaches into a game is a legal grey area.
- CC BY-ND: commercial use allowed, but modified versions cannot be distributed. Converting, decimating or re-texturing for Godot is modification, so impractical.
- CC BY-NC, BY-NC-SA, BY-NC-ND: no commercial use. Never.
- Editorial: no commercial use. Never.
- Standard (paid): no longer purchasable on Sketchfab; those models now sell on Fab.

Credit for CC BY: Sketchfab's template is "This work is based on [model name] by [author] licensed under CC BY 4.0", with links. Its help page only says to put it alongside the published work; a credits screen plus a credits text file shipped with the game is the normal way to meet it (my reading, not Sketchfab's wording). The licence in force is the one at download time, even if the author changes it later, so record model URL, author, licence and date for every download.

**Risks:** 1. Per-item licences: the licence is set per model by the uploader. One wrong tick (NC, ND, SA, Editorial) makes a model unusable. Check each model page and keep a log (URL, author, licence, date, screenshot).

2. Uploader may not own the rights: Sketchfab does not vet uploads. Models ripped from other games, or rebuilt from paid packs, are sometimes posted with a CC tag; a CC licence from someone who does not own the work gives no protection. Avoid anything that looks like it came from a known game, has a new or empty uploader profile, or says "ripped", "extracted" or "port" in the description. Community reports of stolen assets staying up until individually reported exist (anecdotal forum posts, not verified).

3. Brands and trademarks: a CC licence covers the 3D model's copyright only, not the logos or product designs on it. Real gun maker names and markings, car makes, drink and convenience-store brands, railway company liveries and real signage are a separate trademark risk in a sold game. Prefer unbranded models or remove the markings. I could not find any Sketchfab wording addressing this.

4. Scans of real places, artworks and people: museum and heritage scans are often NC or carry extra conditions; scans of recognisable people, modern sculptures or buildings can raise rights beyond the licence. Skip them for a commercial game.

5. Attribution load: every CC BY model needs author, title, licence and link in the credits. Twenty models means twenty credit lines; missing one is a breach.

6. ShareAlike and NoDerivs: both technically permit commercial use but create real problems (licence inheritance; no modified versions). Treat as not usable.

7. Free Standard: the published full licence text describes a paid licence and forbids making the asset extractable as a stand-alone file. A shipped Godot game packs assets, which is the normal reading of compliance, but committing the raw file to a public repository would breach it. The same applies to CC BY in reverse: public repo is fine with credit.

8. AI clauses: the NoAI tag is about feeding models into generative-AI training, not about an AI assistant importing the file into a game. It has no effect on game use. The Standard licence text I read has no AI clause.

9. Change of ownership and terms: Sketchfab moved from Epic to KitBash in August 2026. The licence page still calls Sketchfab a division of Epic while the footer names KitBash, so the legal pages are in transition and terms may be rewritten. Epic said in 2024 that free downloads would eventually end on Sketchfab with notice; no date was ever given and the new owner's plan is not stated. Download and log what is needed sooner rather than later; a licence already granted at download time stays valid.

10. Fab overlap: only CC BY and Standard models could migrate to Fab. The same model may exist on both sites under different terms; use the licence from the site actually downloaded from.

**How to filter:** On sketchfab.com/search: choose Models, turn on "Downloadable", then open the licence filter and tick only "CC0 Public Domain" and "CC Attribution" (optionally "Free Standard"). Leave ShareAlike, NoDerivs, NonCommercial and Editorial unticked.

Licence IDs, confirmed from Sketchfab's own API list (https://api.sketchfab.com/v3/licenses): CC BY = 322a749bcfa841b29dff1e8a1bb74b0b, CC0 = 7c23a1ba438d4306920229c12afcb5f9, Free Standard = 72eb2b1960364637901eacce19283624.

The website search URL pattern I believe works is https://sketchfab.com/search?features=downloadable&licenses=322a749bcfa841b29dff1e8a1bb74b0b&licenses=7c23a1ba438d4306920229c12afcb5f9&type=models&q=<generic term> - but the page returned empty to my fetcher, so the parameter names are unverified; the UI route above is the safe one.

Always re-read the licence line on the individual model page before downloading: the filter is a convenience, the model page is the record. Prefer the glTF/GLB download option for Godot and check the texture resolution and triangle count shown under "Model information".

### Fab (fab.com), including Quixel Megascans and Quixel Megaplants — read live on 12 Nov 2026

YES for a commercially sold Godot game, with one important exception.

- Fab Standard Licence (binding text last updated 1 Oct 2024, still the live text today): commercial use allowed, any engine allowed, shipping the assets inside a sold video game allowed, no credit required. Nothing in the binding EULA text restricts use to Unreal; Fab's own summary says use is not limited to Unreal Engine, and Fab's info page names Godot.
- Megascans: as of 5 Nov 2026 Epic made the whole Megascans library free on Fab (roll-out due to finish 12 Nov 2026). I confirmed live listings showing "Free", "Standard License", and formats fbx / gltf / glb / usdz. So free scanned props, surfaces, rocks, decals and Japanese-themed items are usable in the Godot game.
- Exception 1 (UE-only): Epic's own sample packs (e.g. City Sample Buildings/Vehicles/Crowds) carry a line in the description restricting them to Unreal-based products, even though the Details box says "Standard License". Treat these as NOT usable in Godot. They are also Unreal-format only.
- Exception 2 (format): Megaplants (the new free trees/plants, including Ginkgo, Japanese Cypress, Japanese Medlar) are licensed Standard but ship in Unreal Engine format only, built for Unreal's procedural vegetation editor. Legally allowed in Godot, practically not importable without opening them in Unreal and exporting.
- Other licences on Fab: some listings are CC BY (credit required) and some still carry the legacy "UE Marketplace License" (Unreal only). Check the "License terms" line on every listing.
- Personal tier (the free/cheaper tier) is only for those under USD 100,000 gross revenue in the previous 12 months at the time of acquiring; no upgrade needed if the game later earns more.
- Limited-Time Free: three marketplace products rotate roughly every two weeks (current batch ends 17 Nov 2026); once added to the library while free they stay under the licence in force when acquired.

**Risks:** 1. UE-only trap: Epic-published sample packs (City Sample Buildings, City Sample Vehicles, City Sample Crowds, and likely the other Epic samples) say "UE-Only" in the description while the Details box says "Standard License". A forum bug report about this contradiction has no Epic answer. Do not use these in Godot. Always read the description text, not only the Details box.
2. Legacy licence: listings migrated from the old Unreal Marketplace may still show "UE Marketplace License" under License terms; that one is Unreal-only. Only accept "Standard License" or CC BY.
3. Format trap: many free Fab listings (and all Megaplants) contain Unreal-format files only. Legally fine for Godot under Standard, but no glTF/FBX is provided. Filter by format.
4. Old Bridge downloads: Megascans files obtained years ago through Quixel Bridge under the old Unreal-only terms are not covered; get them again from Fab under the Standard Licence (this point comes from forum posts, not the EULA itself).
5. Extraction clause: the game must restrict players from extracting the assets. Godot PCK files are easy to unpack, so add a line to the game's own EULA forbidding extraction and consider PCK encryption. Do not ship a level editor or mod tool that exports these assets.
6. Repository: assets may only be shared with collaborators in a private repository. The game's Git repository must stay private if Fab files are committed to it.
7. Licence mixing: do not combine Standard Licence assets with GPL or CC BY-SA content/add-ons.
8. CC BY listings on Fab need visible credit; per-item licences vary, so record licence, URL and date for every download.
9. AI flags are per listing: Megascans now shows "Allows usage with AI: Yes"; many other listings are tagged NoAI and may not be fed to generative AI tools (ordinary AI-assisted integration in the engine is not training, but do not use NoAI items as image-generation inputs). Listings also flag "Generated with AI".
10. Change-of-terms history: Megascans was free for all in late 2024, became paid in 2025, and is free again from Nov 2026. Epic's post does not say "permanent". Add wanted items to the library now; acquired items keep the terms in force at acquisition (EULA s.7(a)). Epic does not guarantee re-download, so keep backups.
11. Revenue tier: Personal tier requires under USD 100,000 gross revenue (including funding raised) in the prior 12 months at acquisition time.
12. Memory/size: Megascans scans are high texel density (the checked item is about 5,900 px/m with nine maps). Pick the lowest quality tier offered and downscale to 1K-2K; raw tiers will blow the 8 GB budget and the 100 MB per-file limit.
13. No warranty: marketplace sellers other than Epic give no guarantee their content is clean of third-party rights or real brand logos; weapon and vehicle models from third-party sellers can carry trademarks.

**How to filter:** On fab.com search, these URL parameters worked when tested today:
- Free only: add is_free=1
- glTF only: add asset_formats=gltf (shows a "GLTF" chip)
- Example that returned free Megascans Japanese props: https://www.fab.com/search?q=japanese&is_free=1&asset_formats=gltf
- All Megascans: https://www.fab.com/sellers/Quixel%20Megascans (add ?listing_types=3d-model for models only)
- Rotating free marketplace items: https://www.fab.com/limited-time-free
A seller parameter in the search URL (sellers=...) was dropped by the site, so use the seller page instead. The left filter panel also has a "Licenses" filter.
Then, on each listing, check three things before using it: (a) Details box "License terms" reads "Standard License" (or CC BY, with credit); (b) "Included formats" lists gltf/glb or fbx, not only "Unreal Engine"; (c) the description does not contain the "UE-Only Content" line.

### Meshy (meshy.ai) and Tripo (tripo3d.ai) - AI text/image-to-3D generators, free tiers

The two free tiers differ sharply.

MESHY free plan: usable in a commercially sold Godot game, but only WITH credit. Meshy's terms (last updated 19 Sept 2026) say Meshy owns free-plan output and licenses it to the free user under CC BY 4.0, provided the user credits Meshy. The pricing page labels it "commercial use with attribution". Meshy's suggested credit line is "Model created with Meshy - CC BY 4.0 License" (put it in the game's credits screen and store page/readme). Free allowance: 100 credits per month, up to 10 assets, 10 downloads limited to the "Meshy 6 Lite" model, 1 concurrent task, low queue priority. No engine restriction; Godot is fine. Paid plans (from about GBP 16/month Pro, 1,000 credits) give private, customer-owned assets with no credit required.

TRIPO free plan: NOT usable in a sold game. Tripo's pricing page states the free plan is "Public Models - Non-Commercial Use", and its terms (last updated 11 July 2025), section 5.2.1, say Tripo retains all rights in free users' inputs and outputs. Tripo's own help page says commercial use is "Not granted" to free users. Free allowance: 200 credits per month (about 13 models), 1 concurrent task, 15 exports (H2.5 model only), storage for 10 models, 1 day of edit history. Commercial use needs a paid plan (Pro USD 20/month, 3,000 credits, "Private Models - Commercial Use"); Tripo's help page explicitly lists use in a commercial game on Steam as allowed for paid users. The terms contain no CC BY or attribution clause at all, so the widely repeated claim that Tripo free output is CC BY 4.0 is not supported by Tripo's current terms.

Bottom line: Meshy free = allowed in the paid game with a Meshy credit; Tripo free = not allowed, treat as preview only unless a month of Pro is bought and the assets are generated during that paid period.

**Risks:** 1. Tripo free tier is a licence trap: non-commercial, Tripo keeps all rights, models are public. Many blogs (and some of Tripo's own marketing blog posts) still say "CC BY 4.0, commercial with credit"; the current terms and pricing page say otherwise. Do not ship any Tripo free-tier model.
2. Tripo's help pages contradict each other (one says copyright "belongs entirely to you" but is written for paid users). The terms page is the authoritative one. Whether upgrading later converts earlier free-tier models to commercial is not stated; assume it does not and regenerate while on a paid plan.
3. Meshy free tier: attribution is mandatory (CC BY 4.0), Meshy remains the owner, and the generations are not private. Anyone else may reuse the same model. Keep a log per asset: date, plan, prompt/input image, credit line.
4. Meshy Community page models are a separate trap: the terms mention CC BY-NonCommercial 4.0 for community content (and CC0 for some customer output) and free users cannot download community models anyway. Only use models generated in the owner's own account.
5. AI training: Meshy trains on non-Enterprise content unless opted out by emailing support; Tripo may use free-user content. Neither affects shipping rights, but do not upload anything confidential.
6. Input-image risk on both: the licence covers the 3D output, not the picture fed in. Generating from a photo of a real branded gun, a copyrighted concept image or a trademarked product can still infringe. Use own photos or own-generated concept art; keep real gun maker names/logos off the models.
7. Copyright in purely AI-generated output is legally weak in several countries, so these assets may not be protectable as the owner's exclusive property; and both sets of terms can change (Meshy's were updated Sept 2026), so save a dated copy of the terms on the day of generation.
8. Storefront disclosure: Steam asks developers to declare AI-generated content in the content survey.
9. Quality and technical risk for a realism-first game: raw output is typically very dense (hundreds of thousands to over a million triangles), triangulated with poor edge flow, sometimes non-manifold with flipped normals, auto-unwrapped UVs with many tiny islands, a single material slot, lighting/shadows baked into the colour texture, weak or invented roughness/metal maps, soft melted small details and unreadable text, arbitrary scale and origin, no LODs or collision. Hard-surface items (guns, vehicles, buildings) and first-person-view items look wrong up close. Foliage with thin leaves generates badly.
10. Free-tier quality caps: Meshy free downloads are limited to the Lite model; Tripo free exports are limited to the H2.5 model. The best models and retopology tools are paid.

**How to filter:** There is nothing to filter on a library page; what matters is the plan the model was generated under.
- Meshy: generate inside the owner's own account only (do not take models from the Community page). Free plan output = CC BY 4.0, add the credit "Model created with Meshy - CC BY 4.0 License". Export as GLB (or FBX) for Godot.
- Tripo: free-plan models are not usable in the sold game. Only models generated while on Pro or higher qualify.
Blender cleanup needed before Godot (same for both tools):
1. Import GLB, set real-world scale in metres, put the origin at the base, apply transforms.
2. Merge by distance, recalculate normals outward, delete loose/internal geometry, fix non-manifold parts.
3. Reduce triangles: Decimate for static props (aim roughly 2k-15k triangles), or remesh/retopologise for anything seen close up; then bake the detail of the dense mesh into a normal map on the light mesh.
4. Re-unwrap UVs if the automatic ones are fragmented; rebake the colour texture.
5. Remove baked lighting from the colour map as far as possible, author proper roughness/metal maps, resize textures to 1K-2K.
6. Split material slots where needed, make 2-3 LODs (or let Godot generate them on import), add a simple collision shape.
7. Export GLB, keep each file well under 100 MB.

On Fab, 'Tee Mooring Bollard 30t - Game Ready PBR' (https://www.fab.com/listings/c65ec8c6-5e9a-48b3-8c3c-9210ae0710ce) and 'Shipping Container 20ft ISO - Game Ready PBR' (https://www.fab.com/listings/4eba334e-9304-46bd-a4cd-3a719511d569) by Model Studio are free, FBX, 2K textures, with three LODs (about 19k/8.5k/2.8k triangles) - attractive specs, but the licence line was not shown and the listings disclose they were procedurally generated with AI-written scripts, so treat as unclear until the owner checks the licence when signed in.

## Gap 1 - Japanese city identity: buildings and signage

**Find 1a: PLATEAU (Japanese government 3D city models, incl. Tokyo 23 wards)**
- URL read: https://www.geospatial.jp/ckan/dataset/plateau-tokyo23ku
- Licence as shown on that page: governed by the "PLATEAU Site Policy" section 3 (copyright); the page states the data is free for anyone, commercial use included.
- Secondary confirmation: OpenStreetMap wiki lists it as "CC BY 4.0 or ODbL or ODC-BY (Multi-licensed)" (https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/MLIT_PLATEAU, last edited 2022). A search snippet of MLIT's own start guide names the Public Data Licence v1.0, CC BY 4.0, ODC BY and ODbL.
- UNVERIFIED at source: the official policy page https://www.mlit.go.jp/plateau/site-policy/ did not resolve again today (DNS failure). The exact credit wording must be read there before use.
- Formats and size: FBX about 2.8 GB, OBJ about 2.1 GB for the whole 23-ward set, also CityGML and 3D Tiles. LOD1 (plain boxes) covers all wards; LOD2 (roof shapes) covers about 32 km2 of selected districts.
- Honest fit: not street-level quality. Textures, where present, are aerial photos. Good only as a distant skyline/backdrop and as real Tokyo block footprints and heights to drive the generated mid-rises. The archive is far over the 100 MB per-file limit, so only a small cut-out district, decimated and re-exported as GLB, could be committed. Credit line required.

**Find 1b: Japanese road signs as vector art (Wikimedia Commons)**
- URL read: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Japan_road_sign_302.svg (one of a full numbered series "Japan road sign NNN.svg").
- Licence tag on the page: "This work is in the public domain in Japan according to Article 13 of the Copyright Act".
- Caveats: the tag speaks for Japan only; each file has its own page and tag, so read it per sign. Search results show some route-sign files warn that the lettering is outlined from a specific font and its licence must be checked if the text is changed.
- Fit: very good. SVG renders to small PNG decals at any size, tiny memory cost, and correct signs are a cheap, strong "this is Japan" signal.

**Still missing after this check (needs custom or paid work)**
- Modular Japanese apartment/shopfront kit: still nothing free and realistic. Realistic options are a paid kit, or building facade modules in code/Blender from CC0 materials already found.
- Shop signage and vending-machine faces: must be made new with invented brand names. A Japanese font is needed; Noto Sans JP is the usual choice (SIL Open Font Licence by reputation), but the Google Fonts licence page returned no content today, so UNVERIFIED. Read the OFL.txt inside the download.

## Gap 2 - Hero trees (sakura, Japanese black pine, bamboo)

**Find 2a: EZ-Tree (procedural tree generator, runs in a browser)**
- URLs read: https://github.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree and https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree/main/LICENSE
- Licence: "MIT License", "Copyright (c) 2024 Daniel Greenheck".
- Exports GLB and PNG. The README documents built-in LOD generation: default switch distances 100 and 250 units, about 40% and 20% of triangles, and a GLB export of a LOD tree includes every level.
- Caveats: README names deciduous/oak/ash types only; pine, cherry and bamboo presets are not mentioned. The README does not say whether the bundled bark and leaf images fall under the MIT licence or where they came from, so swap in Poly Haven bark and the scanned leaf atlases from the earlier pass rather than shipping its bundled textures. Realism is mid-level: fine for mid-distance and background trees, not a close-up hero sakura.

**Find 2b: Tree3D for Godot (gdTree3D)**
- URL read: https://github.com/JekSun97/gdTree3D ; reported as MIT by https://gamefromscratch.com/tree3d-procedural-tree-add-on-for-godot/ . The repo page itself links a LICENSE.md I did not get to open, so MIT is second-hand.
- States "Godot 4.5+ (Windows, Linux, MacOS)". Leaves supported. "Automatic LOD" is listed under "Soon...", so no LODs yet. Wind status is contradictory on the page.
- Fit: fallback only until LOD ships.

**Plain statement:** a close-up, blossom-quality sakura, a shaped black pine and a bamboo grove still do not exist free and game-ready. Options in order of quality: (a) Megascans/Megaplants on Fab if the owner confirms the per-item licence while signed in; (b) a paid tree pack or SpeedTree-made assets; (c) custom assembly (EZ-Tree trunk and branches + scanned sakura petal/leaf cards + Poly Haven bark). Bamboo is the easiest to custom-build (straight segmented culms plus leaf cards). AI generators (Meshy/Tripo) are not suitable for foliage.

## Gap 3 - Realistic first-person hands and arms

**Find 3a: Blender Studio "Human Base Meshes" bundle**
- Licence: "available free under a Creative Commons CC0 licence" (https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/06/download-blender-studios-free-human-base-meshes/). A search snippet of blender.org's own asset-bundle page lists v1.4.1, CC0, about 49 MB, updated 20 Jan 2025, Blender 4.2 LTS or newer.

## Record-keeping: what to store with every downloaded asset
Keep one folder per asset and one master list. Suggested layout: a `licenses/` folder in the repository plus `CREDITS.md` and `assets_manifest.csv`.

For each asset, record:
1. Asset name, author/uploader name, and the exact item URL (not the site home page).
2. Licence name and version (e.g. CC0 1.0, CC BY 4.0, Fab Standard) and the URL of the licence text.
3. Date and time of download, and which account was used.
4. Proof of the licence at that moment: a full-page screenshot or saved PDF of the item page showing the licence line, plus an archived copy link (Wayback Machine "Save Page Now"). For Sketchfab also save the item's API record (api.sketchfab.com/v3/models/<id>) as a small JSON file, since it carries the licence field. Licences and listings change or vanish; Sketchfab changed owner in 2026.
5. The original downloaded file, untouched, stored outside the game build (or its SHA-256 hash and file name if it is too large), so the shipped file can be traced to the source.
6. What was changed (decimated, retextured, logos removed, converted to GLB) - CC BY requires noting modifications.
7. The exact credit line to display, copied into `CREDITS.md` and the in-game credits screen. Needed for every CC BY item and for PLATEAU.
8. Any conditions: "no standalone redistribution" (Mixamo, Pixabay, Fab Standard - do not commit these raw to a public repository), trademark or logo clean-up done, and for AI-generated models the tool, plan tier, date and a copy of that plan's terms.
9. Where it is used in the project (file path), so an asset can be pulled quickly if a licence is ever disputed.
10. For Fab/Megascans: the order or library receipt email and a screenshot of the listing's licence field while signed in, since that field did not render for any researcher in this run.

Rule of thumb: if the licence line cannot be seen and captured on the item's own page, do not ship the asset.
